# Extraction benchmark

How well the app reads supplier quotations, measured field by field against a known correct answer.
Run it from `backend/`: `python -m ml.extraction_benchmark --split tune|locked|all` (readings are cached in
`ml/artifacts/extraction_benchmark/`; `--fresh` reads again). The full per-field report is
[backend/ml/artifacts/extraction_benchmark.md](../backend/ml/artifacts/extraction_benchmark.md).

## The data, and what it is not

130 quotations for 26 requests, in `backend/ml/data/quotation_benchmark/`, supplied by the team as `quotation_dataset.zip`.
Its own README says the documents were **generated by code**, not collected from suppliers. They imitate Indian SME
quotations (digital PDFs, scans, phone photos, Excel; swapped columns, GST included or extra, discounts, freight,
deliberate arithmetic mistakes) and each has a complete answer key.

So these numbers measure the reading pipeline on realistic layouts. They are **not** accuracy on real suppliers'
documents. The only real-document figure is the earlier 16 real quotations (96.6 to 96.9% of fields, in
[VALIDATION_REPORT.md](VALIDATION_REPORT.md)), which is too small to split.

## Method

* **Split by request, not by document**, so quotations for the same request never sit on both sides: the 8 requests
  with the lowest hash of their id (30%) are **locked**; the other 18 are **tune**. 90 tune and 40 locked documents.
* Everything was chosen on the tune split. The locked split was read **once**, after the last change to extraction, and
  nothing was changed because of it.
* Scoring: 11 header fields plus quantity, unit price and amount per line. A field that is absent from the document is
  correct only if the extractor leaves it empty, so inventing a value counts as a miss. Numbers match within 0.5 (half
  a rupee or half a unit), dates as ISO strings, and text after normalising case and spacing (equal, or one containing
  the other when longer than 6 characters). These are the dataset's own rules.
* Production path: OCR or text layer, model extraction with one self-correction, and for photos and scans a second
  reading from the page image (kept when its own arithmetic checks are no worse).

## Results

| Split | Documents | Header fields | Line fields | All fields |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tune (used for every choice) | 90 | 98.5% | 98.0% | 98.2% |
| **Locked (read once)** | **40** | **97.0%** | **98.2%** | **97.7%** |

Locked split by format: digital PDF 99.8% (20), Excel 100% (5), photo 98.9% (7), **scanned PDF 89.6% (8)**. The
format groups are small; one bad scan moves a group by several points.

### How the tune number got there (same 90 documents)

| Step | All fields |
|---|---:|
| Starting point: reading the OCR text | 84.9% |
| Photos and scans read from the page image instead | 95.8% |
| Clearer field definitions (supplier's own quotation number, freight only when charged, delivery time as printed, payment terms not bank details), dates such as "25-Oct-26", delivery phrases such as "4-6 weeks" | 98.2% |

To get 98.2%, nine documents were read a second time. Seven had hit the free-tier rate limit during a parallel test run
and fallen back to weaker models; two came back empty from the page-image reading. The app itself now retries an
empty page-image reading once, so that part is real behaviour. The rate-limit part is a real weakness (below).

## Arithmetic checks

11 of the 130 quotations contain the supplier's own arithmetic mistake (a line amount or grand total that does not add
up, by ₹100 to ₹1,455). Before this round the checks used a 2% tolerance and sent **none** of them for review. Now the
tolerance is ₹2 or 0.01%, and a total that does not add up always sends the quotation for review:

| | Sent for review |
|---|---:|
| Quotations with a supplier's arithmetic mistake | **11 of 11** (was 0) |
| Correct quotations | 20 of 119; 17 of these carry a discount, which was not yet extracted, so the total could not be reconciled |

## Discounts (added after the locked run)

Line and quotation discounts are now extracted as printed and reconciled in code. Because this changes extraction, it
can only be measured on the tune split; the locked split has already been used and is not read again for it.

The re-read of the 90 tune quotations ran after Gemini's free daily quota was spent, so every reading came from the
backup models (Groq, NVIDIA). On that run:

| | Result |
|---|---:|
| Quotation discounts read to the rupee | 11 of 13 |
| Discounted quotations still sent for review | 4 of 13 (13 of 13 before) |
| Supplier arithmetic mistakes sent for review | 8 of 8 |
| Correct quotations sent for review | 15 of 82 |
| All fields | **89.0%** (98.2% with Gemini, before the discount fields) |

The 89.0% mixes two changes, the backup models and the new fields, so it is not a result for either alone. The
per-document errors were mostly misread digits (87 read as 85, 1,56,672 as 1,56,720), typical of the weaker models. A
re-read with Gemini, once its quota resets, will separate the two; until then the tune figure with discounts is
**not measured**.

Because of this run, a reading produced by a backup model is now always sent for review.

## Limits

* Code-generated documents; no claim about real suppliers' documents beyond the 16 real ones.
* Scanned PDFs are the weakest format on the locked split (89.6% on 8 documents).
* Under free-tier limits, reading falls back to weaker models and accuracy drops (93% when tests ran alongside; 89.0%
  with the main model's daily quota spent). Such readings are sent for review, and a cap on concurrent model calls
  (`AI_MAX_CONCURRENT_CALLS`) limits bursts; neither restores the accuracy itself.
* Handwriting is not in this benchmark.
* Time per document: median 19 s, slowest 159 s (a rate-limited read).
